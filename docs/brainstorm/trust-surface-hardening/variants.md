# Trust-Surface Hardening — variants

The three variants below are reproduced verbatim from the consultant reply stored at `cli-output/consultant.md` (see the provenance note at the end for why that file and not `claude.md`).

### Variant 1: Substrate-first consolidation (shared crypto and seal modules, reject-at-write)

- **Approach**: Sequence the wave around two shared modules built before their consumers. First `src/core/brain/secrets/envelope.ts`: the scrypt KDF (`scryptSync`/`timingSafeEqual` from node:crypto, explicit `N/r/p/maxmem`, random salt, parameters recorded inside a versioned envelope, wrong-passphrase detected by the GCM tag), the wrapped-keyfile format that replaces the raw 32-byte keyfile on opt-in (card 1), and the bundle envelope of card 3; card 2 then routes through the same substrate's decrypt with a read-only resolve variant so `$secret:` resolution avoids the `resolveSecretForExec` writer side (last_used_at stamp plus custody audit). Second, lift `sealManifest`/`manifestVerifies` (`src/core/state/migrate.ts:186-194`) into `src/core/integrity/digest.ts` beside `sha256Hex`/`canonicalJson` so both adoption-plan seals (card 7) and the extraction-contract fingerprint (card 9) pass through one primitive. Cards 4 and 6 reject at write time - the composers refuse tag values failing the `src/core/tags.ts` rule and capture refuses an undeclared kind before `allocateAndCreate` - and cards 5 and 8 extend the existing response envelopes additively (`digest_count`/`divergent` on both single-session reads, a `note` key on the CLI `{ ok: true }` envelope).
- **Trade-offs**:
  - Pros: one spelling of the KDF and one of the seal matches the DRY posture `src/core/tags.ts:8-11` already demands of the tag rule; card 3 shares the KDF with card 1 by card statement, so the substrate is load-bearing rather than speculative; rejection-before-allocation reuses the existing typed-refusal shapes (`CaptureContractError`, `SchemaVocabularyError`) and the guard ordering `store.ts:119-124` already mandates.
  - Cons: hard rejection on topic shape breaks live MCP/CLI feedback and any lane that writes `brain/topic/foo bar` today until callers slugify; rejecting capture kinds in vaults whose packs were authored before `brain-capture` existed needs a compatibility story for the Telegram lane; the up-front substrate couples the review and landing order of three otherwise independent secrets slices.
- **Complexity**: large
- **Risk**: high

### Variant 2: Independent slices (detect-only governance, dedicated divergence surfaces)

- **Approach**: Ship nine vertical slices with no module extracted first: the wrapped keyfile, the bundles and the resolver each carry their own KDF/envelope code locally with convergence deferred, and cards 7 and 9 implement sealing in place. Cards 4 and 6 are detection-only: an opt-in `frontmatter-tags` hygiene detector registered through `HYGIENE_DETECTOR_IDS` (`src/core/brain/hygiene/types.ts:12-20`) and modeled on `detectTags`, plus a doctor finding for vaults whose declared `page_types` omit the kinds the build stamps; no composer or capture writer changes. Cards 5 and 8 add new dedicated surfaces - a session-summary divergences read and a payload-parse diagnostics output - leaving `brain_session_summary` get, the CLI get and the four parse sites byte-identical.
- **Trade-offs**:
  - Pros: zero write-path break risk - topics with spaces keep landing, Telegram captures keep flowing, every existing test pin survives untouched; each slice reviews, lands and reverts independently; no read-path consumer or test churn on the current envelopes.
  - Cons: three private copies of envelope/KDF code is drift in exactly the layer where a mistake costs credentials, against the wave's own DRY constraint; detection-only leaves the trust surface documented rather than hardened - the silent sorted-last read at `session-summary.ts:179` stays silent on the primary surface and the agent must know to call the new one; two new surfaces cost command-manifest, help-text, census and MCP-parity work that additive keys avoid.
- **Complexity**: medium
- **Risk**: low

### Variant 3: Forced sharing only, tiered enforcement, additive named keys

- **Approach**: Share exactly what the cards force: one `src/core/brain/secrets/envelope.ts` KDF/wrapped-envelope module under cards 1 and 3 (card 3 shares the KDF with card 1 by statement), with card 2 as a separate resolver-plane slice that composes `parseSecretReference` with a read-oriented decrypt over the custody store and threads resolved literals into `EGRESS_REDACTION_OPTIONS`/`redactConfigMapping` so `redactKnownSecretValues` finally gains production callers; likewise one `sealWithDigest`/`digestVerifies` lift into `src/core/integrity/digest.ts` under cards 7 and 9. Enforcement is tiered by blast radius: card 4 rejects caller-controlled `extraTags` and slugify-or-rejects topic inside the composers (consuming the rule from `src/core/tags.ts`, never a copy), while card 6 fails open on absent config per the `readSchemaPackSource` substitution precedent (`schema-pack.ts:56`) and rejects an undeclared `kind` only when a pack explicitly declares `page_types`, with an opt-in hygiene detector as the companion for existing vaults; lock/unlock stays off MCP per the `brain_secrets` set/get refusal (admin-tools.ts:295-299). Cards 5 and 8 extend the existing envelopes additively with named tokens - `digest_count`, conditional `divergent` plus an id/hash list rather than full echoes to respect `MCP_PREVIEW_BUDGET`, a `summary-divergent`-style reason token in the `payload_mismatch`/`coverage-divergent` grammar, and a `note` key on the CLI envelope when the shared payload parser stripped a think block - and fold the pre-existing CLI serializer drift (missing `project`) into the same wave.
- **Trade-offs**:
  - Pros: the only centralized code is the two substrates the cards genuinely share, so review order stays slice-sized; rejection lands where the caller controls the input (extraTags) and where the vault made a declaration (page_types), matching the write-session validator's existing polarity (fail closed on declared-but-unknown at `validate.ts:181`, fail open on absent vocabulary) while detectors cover vaults that predate the declarations; additive named keys follow the conditional-key idiom, cannot trip the MCP output contract (the tool declares no outputSchema), and keep byte-identity when inactive.
  - Cons: three enforcement polarities in one wave (reject-always on extraTags, reject-only-when-declared on capture kinds, detect-only companion) need careful documentation so operators can predict which writes refuse; the resolver's store-lock-on-config-paths question and card 3's honest `EGRESS_SITES` declaration (ciphertext defeats both `sharedRedactor` and `noVaultContent` as-is) remain open decisions inside otherwise-settled slices.
- **Complexity**: medium
- **Risk**: medium

### Recommended: Variant 3

**Rationale**: It centralizes only the two substrates the cards force to be shared - card 3 shares its KDF with card 1 by statement, and cards 7 and 9 both reduce to `sha256Hex(canonicalJson(...))` over a canonical body - unlike Variant 1, which couples all three secrets slices up front, or Variant 2, which leaves three drifting copies of credential-critical code. Its tiered enforcement places fail-closed behavior exactly where the repo's own precedents sit (declared-vocabulary rejection with fail-open on absent config, caller-controlled-input rejection at the composers) and covers pre-existing vaults through detectors instead of breaking Telegram capture or live feedback. Additive named keys on existing envelopes are the established house grammar, preserve byte-identity when the features are inactive, and avoid Variant 2's split-surface problem where the primary single-session read stays silent.

## Orchestrator decision

**Picked: Variant 3** — the consultant's recommendation, accepted without override. The two substrates it centralizes are exactly the ones the cards force to be shared (the secrets envelope across cards 1 and 3; the digest seal across cards 7 and 9), its enforcement polarities sit on the repo's own precedents, and its additive named keys keep every inactive feature byte-identical. The full acceptance rationale is in `design.md` ("Chosen approach"); the tiered polarities are specified per write site in "Design decisions".

**Provenance note.** This wave ran in host mode: the external CLI consultant was replaced by an in-session subagent running against the same prompt (`cli-output/prompt.md`), so the reply is stored at `cli-output/consultant.md` rather than `claude.md`. The variants above are that reply, verbatim.
